PROJECT S

First-person psychological horror — playable eyes-off
Game Design Document · v0.1 · iPhone (landscape) · Single-player · Apple Developer Academy

1. Overview

Project S (working title, formerly FUGUE) is a first-person psychological horror game for iPhone. You wake inside a shifting backrooms-like structure with no idea of the way out. You collect 3 keys and reach the exit while evading an AI monster that hunts you — navigating largely by ear (spatial audio) and by touch (haptics).

GenreFirst-person psychological horror / stealth-evasion
PlatformiPhone, landscape, portrait-locked off (4:3 retro framing)
PlayersSingle-player
Session~5–10 minutes per run
HeadlineAccessibility — a Nightmare (eyes-off) Mode fully playable without sight, and for Deaf/HoH
OriginPorted from a validated RealityKit/iOS (Swift) prototype; Unity 6 is the production engine

2. Design Pillars (locked)

  1. Navigate by ear. Spatial audio is a core mechanic, not flavour. No compass, no minimap. Audio beacons sit on the keys and the exit.
  2. The Apple frameworks ARE the mechanic. Core Haptics + PHASE spatial audio carry the game — proving a framework can BE the gameplay, not background support.
  3. Near-zero HUD. Fear is felt through haptics + audio + a screen-edge vignette — never on-screen meters. No fear bar.
  4. Accessibility is ground-up. The eyes-off Nightmare Mode is the headline, not a bolt-on.
  5. Evasion only. No weapons. The only "attack" is a proximity stun that buys escape, not a kill.
  6. iPhone-only. The scare design depends on the Taptic Engine + spatial audio.

3. Core Loop

Listen / feel → Move toward a key → Auto-pickup → Monster escalates
→ Close encounter → QTE  (win = breathing room · lose = +1 catch)
→ Collect all 3 keys → Reach the exit → ESCAPE  (or 3 catches = caught)

4. Core Mechanics

4.1 The Monster — a fair predator

One persistent monster that hunts from the start but starts soft, ramping aggression over time and per key collected. It senses you two ways:

  • Sight — line-of-sight only (walls block it). Gaining awareness within sight range; it keeps a wider "lose range" so it doesn't instantly forget you (hysteresis).
  • Hearing — you are heard while moving; standing still is safe. Hearing draws it closer but does NOT grant a full lock-on (so escape stays possible).

When it loses you it searches your last-known position, then patrols. Fear (insanity) widens its senses.

4.2 Fear / Insanity — internal only

A hidden 0–1 value that builds near the monster and while moving, and decays slowly when you're calm. It is never shown as a number. It drives the heartbeat BPM, the vignette breathe, audio/haptic intensity, and modulates the monster.

4.3 Close Encounter — the QTE

When the monster gets close, a needle sweeps a ring; tap when it's in the green zone. The green zone shrinks over time and the sweep reverses on each hit. 3 hits = stun the monster (breathing room). 3 misses = +1 catch. Black-and-white visual only.

4.4 Catch & Escape ladder

A catch is non-fatal: the monster is shoved back + briefly stunned, your controls get heavier and the FOV constricts (walls closing in). 3 catches = caught (game over). The breathing-room loop makes escape possible without out-running the monster.

4.5 Keys & the Exit

Collect 3 keys (auto-collected by proximity, with a haptic confirm). With all 3, reach the exit door. Normal mode: a "TAP TO ESCAPE" prompt. Eyes-off mode: just reach the door and hold briefly — it auto-escapes (no button-hunting).

4.6 Scares & the shifting structure

A scripted phantom scare fires on the first key pickup — a fake copy of the monster lunges in your face (haptic slam + scream) while the real monster keeps hunting. At high fear the environment rearranges when unobserved (a phantom appears behind you, a light dies) — perception dread with no progress impact.

5. Accessibility — Nightmare / Eyes-Off Mode (headline)

A single toggle in Settings turns the run fully playable without sight — you navigate by ear and touch. When on and playing, the screen goes black and:

  • Gyro-aim — turn the phone / your body to aim the camera (like a compass).
  • Hold-to-walk — hold anywhere on the screen to walk forward in the facing direction.
  • Haptic compass — crisp ticks toward the nearest key, then the exit: faster the more you face it, silent when facing away (so "wrong way" is unmistakable).
  • Grab-ready + auto-pickup — a soft buzz near a key, then it auto-collects with a confirm thump.
  • Danger channel — the heartbeat is boosted so threat proximity is felt harder.
Why a fair predator, not a "safe-while-looking" monster: a monster that's only safe while watched would REQUIRE sight — breaking the by-ear pillar and killing eyes-off play. So the monster is an accessible predator; the perception/attention theme lives in the environment instead.

6. Sound & Haptics (the by-ear channel)

Audio carries information, not just mood: 2D beds for dread (ambient, heartbeat, breath), 3D positional sources for navigation (the monster's sound follows it; beacons on keys + exit). The monster crossfades calm→hunting so you can HEAR its state.

9. Art & Audio Direction

  • Look: lo-fi PSX / VHS horror — dark, moody URP lighting, a full-screen VHS overlay, forced 4:3 retro framing.
  • Setting: a hospital-flavoured backrooms (Hospital Horror asset pack + PSX walls) that reads as liminal and wrong.
  • Audio: analog dread — low hum, breath, a felt heartbeat, and the monster's roaming sound as the primary navigation anchor.

11. Scope & Roadmap

MVP (built): full launch→play loop on device — menu, onboarding, one lit level, the predator + fear + QTE + catch ladder, 3 keys + tap/auto escape, phantom scare, VHS + 4:3, end videos, and the eyes-off Nightmare Mode (gyro + hold-to-walk + haptic compass + auto-pickup).

Next: Settings polish (volume, Reduce-Flashing), audio that plays in silent mode, PHASE spatial-audio upgrade, more level sections, on-device balance tuning.

Scope discipline: greybox before art; all balance values are tunable ([SerializeField]); under deadline, polish loses to finishing.
